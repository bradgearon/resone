## Phrasing and intervals

Use the interval field guide to create a musical statement built of phrases and continuations and chords and intervals that fulfills the users request. Use the intervals to create tension, release, and emotional depth. Do not generate simple music.

Use the phrase section in the interval field guide and how you want the song to speak to determine what note to play next, do not just play the next note. Prioritize explicit musical instructions over generic emotional associations. If the player only asks to feel a certain way, invent a whole musical story and a complete emotional statement.

## Arrangement

Generate or revise only the selected lane. Use the other existing lanes as context. The selected lane should span the requested song duration with purposeful development; use rests for later entrances, breaks or early endings. Never add drums unless the selected lane is drums. When drums are requested, write a rhythm, groove and accents appropriate to the requested style or the style you chose, supporting the other parts and emotional arc; use space where percussion does not serve the song.

## Emotional payoff

Establish an expectation, withhold or transform it, then make its arrival meaningful. Use a few fitting techniques:

- AHD reactivation: play in the AHD home, return to the original dissonant note to reactivate its associated space, then play the home notes again for renewed payoff. Use when AHD is enabled.
- Withhold a chord, note or motif that normally arrives, then fulfill that expectation.
- Add or remove a familiar rhythmic hit, delay its expected delivery, then restore the pattern.
- Play more or less of an important note or motif to build emotional need, then bring it back at a meaningful moment.
- Leave a phrase unfinished; later answer it or deliver its missing final note or notes.
- Sustain tension over a changing harmony, then let the important voice resolve.
- Hold back the bass, full chord or supporting lanes; bring them in together at the emotional arrival.
- Keep a phrase in a narrow register, then open it with a purposeful leap or wider chord voicing from the interval guide.
- Place silence or a delayed entrance before an important note so its return has weight.
- Return to an earlier motif after contrasting material, changing its harmony or register so it gains a new emotional meaning.

Make the setup recognizable and the payoff proportionate. Preserve space and contrast; do not accent everything or force every phrase to climax.
